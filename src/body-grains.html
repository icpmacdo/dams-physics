A house stands on loose sand that is full of water. An earthquake shakes the ground for a few seconds, too fast for the water to drain away. What happens to the house?

It sinks and tilts. Shaking makes loose grains try to settle into the gaps between them. The water in those gaps can’t escape in time, so its pressure rises until it carries the weight the grains were carrying. With nothing pressing the grains together, friction between them vanishes and the sand flows like a heavy liquid. That is liquefaction.
