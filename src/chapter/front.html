A chapter on how dams stand and fail

Dams in Section

A dam is a pile of soil or a block of concrete holding back water. Whether it stands or fails comes down to one idea you already know from the beach: sand is strong when its grains press together, and water pressure pushes them apart.

This chapter builds that idea one page at a time, then uses it to cut real dams open. It starts with a handful of sand and ends with a dam you design and test yourself. It assumes no engineering background.

How to read it

  • Read the pages in order. Each one takes one idea and has at most one drawing.
  • Most drawings are live: each page tells you which control to use and what you should see.
  • Some pages end with a question. Make your guess, then turn the page for the answer.
  • Every new word is in bold where it is first explained. The glossary at the end collects them.
  • Use the buttons at the bottom of each page, or the arrow keys, to move between pages.

Contents

Nine parts. The first three are about soil and water. The rest apply them to dams.
