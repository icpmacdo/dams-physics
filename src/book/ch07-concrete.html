Seepage under a concrete dam: uplift

A concrete dam is the block on the water cushion from . Water seeping under its base pushes up on it, and that uplift cancels part of the dam's weight. The weight, through friction on the rock, is what stops the dam sliding. The usual remedy is a row of drain holes bored into the rock from a tunnel inside the dam (the gallery), just downstream of the grout curtain, to let the water out. A common design assumption, used by the US Bureau of Reclamation among others, is that working drains cut the pressure at the drain line to the tailwater (the water level downstream) plus one-third of the difference between reservoir and tailwater.
