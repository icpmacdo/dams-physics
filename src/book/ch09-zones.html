A large embankment dam is rarely made of one material. It is zoned: built from several soils and rocks, each placed where it does one job. A zone holds water back, or lets water out without letting soil out, or adds weight. The clay core is the clay plug from , taking almost the whole drop in head. Filters and drains let the water that gets through escape, so the pressure downstream of the core stays low. That leaves the downstream shell fairly dry, its grains pressing hard on each other, which is where its grip comes from (). The upstream shell sits under the reservoir and stays soaked, but it is free-draining, so its water pressure is only what the reservoir puts there. Read any section from the middle outward and you'll find the same order: tight in the centre, coarser and more free-draining toward the faces.
